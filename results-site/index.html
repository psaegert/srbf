About these numbers

The 2026-09 release is a fresh srbf run: every method sees the same laws, sampled the same way, and every answer is read the same way.

The 2026-07 release re-scores the archived predictions behind the Flash-ANSR paper.

Problems

29 catalogs hold 6,660 laws, and each law is one problem: 512 support points for the method and 512 validation points it never sees, drawn from the catalog's own ranges without noise. The numbers on this page come from one draw per problem.

Judging an answer

An answer is read twice. Numerically, by its fraction of variance unexplained on the validation points. Symbolically, by simplifying the prediction and the law into one certified canonical form, in the law's own variable names, and comparing the two. How a method arrives at the answer it submits is its own affair: the explorer states that rule next to the method's name.

Budget and cost

A method runs along a ladder of budgets \(1, 2, 4, \dots\) per problem: samples, beam width or candidates, and search iterations for PySR. Cost is seconds per problem on one reference machine, the only timing this release publishes, so a curve is drawn against that time or against the candidate budget. PySR spends iterations rather than candidates and therefore appears on the time axis only.

Pooling and intervals

A pooled number is a statistic over all laws of the selected catalogs. It is shown for a budget once the method has finished every selected catalog there, so every point, row and rank is over the same laws. A rate counts every law, and a failed prediction is a miss; a continuous metric describes the successful predictions. Intervals are 95 %: a t-interval for a mean, an order-statistic interval for a median, and for a rate \(\hat{p}\) over \(K\) laws the Wilson score interval

$$ \frac{\hat{p} + \frac{z^2}{2K} \;\pm\; z \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})}{K} + \frac{z^2}{4K^2}}} {1 + \frac{z^2}{K}}, \qquad z = 1.96 . $$

Provenance

The series are re-scored through srbf.analysis from the archived raw predictions of the Flash-ANSR v23.0 evaluation, the experiments behind Breaking the Simplification Bottleneck in Amortized Neural Symbolic Regression. Flash-ANSR series carry version v23.0 (v23.3 for the KV-cache decode re-evaluation), and the version travels with each series in the legend. The Ablations group holds the paper's ablation grid: training data and budget (Track A), architecture reverts toward NeSymReS (Track B), the constants prior (C1) and inference-only re-evaluations of the unchanged checkpoint (Track Z). Ablation B1 (post-norm) has no timing in its archive and so no place on the compute axis.

Benchmarks and sweeps

FastSRB is a curated suite of textbook and physics expressions, each evaluated at several sampled problem instances. v23-val is a held-out set of expression skeletons from Flash-ANSR's training distribution, decontaminated against the training data. Three sweeps supply the x axis: inference compute, support size and noise level. The compute axis is the median wall-clock fit time per problem, the one budget that is comparable across methods: each method scales along its own knob (sampled candidates, iterations, beam width), and time puts them on a single frontier. Times were measured on the same hardware per sweep.

From predictions to a point

  1. Placeholder rows are excluded. A draw that could not be realized, for example because no valid problem instance exists at the requested support size, never enters a statistic.
  2. Failures count against rate metrics only. A problem without a usable prediction scores 0 on recovery, exact match and success. Metrics that need a prediction (FVU, token overlap, edit distances, lengths, timings) are taken over the successful predictions.
  3. Rows are grouped by expression. The draws of one expression are averaged into one value, so no expression is over-weighted.
  4. The per-expression values are bootstrapped. Over 1,000 resamples with replacement, the plotted point is the median of the resampled means and the band is its 2.5th to 97.5th percentile. The n in a tooltip (tap or hover) is the number of contributing expressions.

Reading the curves

Recovery and FVU are scored against clean targets: in the noise sweep a method fits noisy values and is evaluated on the noise-free function, because the question is whether the law was recovered. Token-level and exact-match metrics compare raw prefix skeletons, which the methods spell differently, so their levels are offset between method families and the signal is the trend within a series. Log-probability and selection score come from Flash-ANSR's own decoder and selection stage, and the baselines have no equivalent.

Who chose each configuration

Every method carries a label that answers the first question to ask of a benchmark whose authors enter their own method: who picked the hyperparameters? The labels are part of the written fairness policy and are shown wherever methods are compared.

  • Upstream defaults (PySR, NeSymReS, E2E): the method's own released defaults, untouched in either direction. Where a default is consequential here, srbf measures the consequence and documents it next to the method's results.
  • Author-blessed (every Flash-ANSR series): the configuration was chosen by the method's authors. The benchmark and Flash-ANSR share authors, which is exactly what this label discloses; any method's authors can submit a blessed configuration on the same terms.
  • Maintainer-chosen (the prior reference): a benchmark-native series with no upstream and no external author.

Paired comparisons

Every method solved the same laws, so two methods are compared law by law, \(\Delta_k = m_A^{(k)} - m_B^{(k)}\), rather than through two separate averages: the difficulty both share widens each average's interval and cancels in the difference. Two intervals can overlap while one method is reliably better law after law, so an A-versus-B question belongs in a paired view.

Rates

For a rate, only the laws the two methods disagree on carry information: \(b\) laws that A alone recovers and \(c\) that B alone recovers, out of \(K\). The exact McNemar test needs no asymptotics, and the difference of the rates carries a 95 % Wald interval:

$$ p = \min\!\Big\{1,\; 2\, P\big[\operatorname{Bin}(b + c, \tfrac{1}{2}) \le \min(b, c)\big]\Big\}, \qquad \hat{\Delta} = \frac{b - c}{K} \;\pm\; \frac{z}{K} \sqrt{b + c - \frac{(b - c)^2}{K}} . $$

Continuous metrics

Over the laws where both methods have a finite value, the view reports the mean difference \(\bar{\Delta}\) with its 95 % interval and a two-sided exact sign test on the wins and losses. Ratios and times are differenced on a log scale and read as factors: \(\times\, 0.5\) is half. These are contrasts from one draw per problem, on the same laws for both methods.

Convention

Δ = selected − baseline; in the Matrix, cells read row − column, and the tap or hover record says which series a value favors. The Absolute Table answers what one method achieves at a budget; the Paired views (the Δ-curves and the verdict Matrix) answer who is better. Every statistic is computed ahead of time by srbf's open-source paired layer and published with the page, so what is shown is exactly what is citable. Every claim is per benchmark: the two benchmarks measure different populations, and there is deliberately no combined number.

The noise margin

Each comparison is judged against a measurement-noise margin: the largest difference to expect between two equally good models, given the draw-to-draw noise of these two series. The margin is derived from the data: each model's repeated draws are split into halves, the halves are compared with each other, and the margin is the 95th percentile of those null differences, combined for the pair at hand.

Budgets

Verdicts are issued at standardized compute budgets (≤1, 10, 100 or 1000 seconds per problem, median), and every method is evaluated at exactly that time: interpolated per problem, linearly in log-time, between its two bracketing measured configurations, which is the model that draws the Δ(t) curves. A method whose cheapest configuration exceeds the budget is n/a. A method whose measurements end below it is never extrapolated: its largest tested configuration is carried forward, flagged max tested and treated as a lower bound, and a verdict that such a side could overturn by improving is downgraded to undecided (max-tested limit). Same-method comparisons (ablations, the size ladder) keep the same measured configuration on both sides instead, because equal time would let two factors vary at once. Between the marked budgets the slider shows descriptive reads of the plotted curves; verdicts and corrected p-values belong to the pre-declared marks, which keeps the set of quotable claims finite.

Verdicts

  • better / worse: the 95 % confidence interval clears the noise margin. A real difference, larger than benchmark noise can explain.
  • equivalent: the interval fits entirely inside the margin. Any remaining difference is smaller than this benchmark can measure, which is not proof that the models are identical.
  • undecided: neither of the above. Each record reports its minimum detectable effect (MDE₈₀), the smallest true difference this comparison would have caught 80 % of the time; smaller differences could have been missed, so undecided never means equal.

Corrected p-values

Checking many comparisons at once produces false positives by luck alone, so displayed significance is corrected: Holm within the small pre-declared set of confirmatory comparisons, Benjamini–Hochberg for everything else, which stays labeled exploratory. The confirmatory set (a small C in the matrix) varies exactly one factor per pair: each ablation against its parent, adjacent steps of the size ladder, and each model size against each baseline. The p-value asks whether Δ is exactly zero and the verdict asks whether the interval clears the margin, so a comparison can be significant and still undecided. Verdicts do not change with the correction toggle, which can show raw p-values for browsing; the corrected values are the quotable ones.

Measurement details

The compute axis pairs configurations whose median cost is t, not per-expression equal budgets; solid dots are measured configurations, hollow dots are interpolated between measured neighbors. Where one model failed on an expression the other solved, rate metrics count the failure as 0 while quality-of-fit metrics compare only the expressions both solved, and each record discloses those counts. On v23-val, cross-method cells carry an in-distribution note, because that benchmark is drawn from Flash-ANSR's training distribution. Bands on Δ-curves are pointwise. Every number carries a release id; cite them together.

Ranks

Ranks answer the standings question: how do all methods compare at once? Within each law the methods are placed 1st, 2nd, and so on by the chosen metric, and a method's standing is its mean rank over the \(K\) laws, \(\bar{r}_j = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k} r_{jk}\).

What a rank measures

Every law hands out the same places, so easy and hard laws count alike, and a win by a hair counts like a win by a mile. Ranks measure consistency where the paired views measure by how much, which is why the two complement each other. Methods with the same value share the mean of the places they occupy. A method without a usable answer for a law is placed last on it, on every ranking metric. Failures rank last within their expression on quality metrics; expression-property metrics such as length are ranked over the expressions every method solved, a smaller, labeled population.

Groups

Friedman's test first asks whether the spread of mean ranks is larger than interchangeable methods would produce by chance; groups are drawn only if it is. For \(k\) methods, the critical difference (Nemenyi, 5 %)

$$ \mathrm{CD} = q_{0.05} \sqrt{\frac{k (k + 1)}{6 K}} $$

is the smallest gap between two mean ranks that counts as real when every pair is compared at once. Methods closer than one CD share a shaded band: no reliable difference was detected at this sample size, which is not proof of equality.

Comparisons won

The share of head-to-head comparisons a method wins, a tie counting as half, is the mean rank on a fixed scale: \(w_j = (k - \bar{r}_j) / (k - 1)\). At 100 % a method beats every other on every law and at 50 % it breaks even, whatever the number of ranked methods, so it can be followed along the budget while the roster changes.

Holding methods equal

The ranking follows the selection: any set of methods over any set of catalogs. The methods are held equal either on the rung of their own ladder or on time, each at the largest finished budget that the reference machine timed within the chosen limit per problem. A method that cannot finish within the limit sits out, and so does one without outcomes against the others at that setting. A method whose ladder ends below the limit is drawn hollow: more budget could only help it.

Roster and budget

The fixed roster, every Flash-ANSR size plus every baseline, is ranked at exactly the selected compute budget. Hollow dots mark methods whose measurements end below the budget: their plotted position is a worst case that more compute could only improve. A pre-declared Paired verdict can separate two methods that share a band, because it asks a different, magnitude-based question.

Primary metric

One ranking is primary and quotable, the one on held-out \(\log_{10}\) FVU. The ranking on any other metric is exploratory, and a badge on the metric control says which kind is on screen.

Metrics

A problem is a law with prefix skeleton \(\bar{\tau}\) (constants masked, structure simplified with SimpliPy), a support set \((X, y)\) of \(N\) points given to the method, and a disjoint, equally sized validation set \((X_{\mathrm{val}}, y_{\mathrm{val}})\) it never sees. A method returns an expression with skeleton \(\hat{\tau}\) and fitted constants; \(\hat{y}\) and \(\hat{y}_{\mathrm{val}}\) are its values on the two sets, \(|\tau|\) is a skeleton's length in prefix tokens, and \(K\) is the number of problems. The definitions follow the Flash-ANSR paper; in the explorer every metric carries a one-sentence definition behind its ?.

Numeric fit

All numeric metrics build on the Fraction of Variance Unexplained, the scale-invariant normalized squared error

$$ \operatorname{FVU}(y, \hat{y}) \;=\; \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} (y_i - \bar{y})^2}, \qquad \bar{y} = \tfrac{1}{N} \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{N} y_i . $$

An FVU of 0 is a perfect fit, an FVU of 1 is no better than predicting the mean, and values above 1 are worse than the mean predictor. Because the denominator is the variance of the targets, the measure is invariant to the output scale of the problem. The implementation guards the numerics: a divergent or non-finite prediction scores \(\infty\), and residuals are rescaled internally to avoid floating-point over- and underflow.

Numeric recovery (vNRR) higher is better

$$ \mathrm{vNRR} \;=\; \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbb{1}\!\left[ \operatorname{FVU}\!\big(y_{\mathrm{val}}^{(k)}, \hat{y}_{\mathrm{val}}^{(k)}\big) \le \varepsilon_{32} \right], \qquad \varepsilon_{32} = 2^{-23} \approx 1.19 \times 10^{-7} $$

The validation Numeric Recovery Rate, the headline metric. A problem counts as recovered only if the predicted expression matches the clean function values on the unseen validation points up to 32-bit floating-point machine precision. This is a deliberately strict, essentially all-or-nothing criterion: an expression either is the (numeric) ground truth on the domain or it is not. Approximate fits, however close, do not count. Failed predictions count as 0.

Numeric recovery on support (fNRR) higher is better

The same machine-precision indicator evaluated on the support points the method was fitted on, \(\mathbb{1}[\operatorname{FVU}(y, \hat{y}) \le \varepsilon_{32}]\). Comparing fNRR to vNRR separates memorization of the support from generalization to the rest of the domain: an expression that nails the support but misses validation is an overfit, not a recovery.

log10 FVU (validation / support) lower is better

$$ \log_{10} \operatorname{FVU}\big(y_{\mathrm{val}}, \hat{y}_{\mathrm{val}}\big) \quad\text{and}\quad \log_{10} \operatorname{FVU}\big(y, \hat{y}\big) $$

The continuous companion to the recovery rate: how close the fits are on a log scale, including approximate fits the strict \(\varepsilon_{32}\) threshold discards. A value of \(-2\) means 99% of the target variance is explained; \(-7\) is at the recovery threshold. Both are diagnostics over successful predictions only, and cover fits with finite, positive FVU: an exact \(\operatorname{FVU} = 0\) maps to \(-\infty\) and drops that expression from this aggregate, and a divergent fit (\(\operatorname{FVU} = \infty\)) likewise drops out. Note that the paper goes further and excludes all numerically perfect fits (\(\operatorname{FVU} \le \varepsilon_{32}\)) from its log-FVU summaries; this page keeps near-perfect fits with tiny positive FVU, so its levels are not directly comparable to the paper's.

R² floored at 0 (validation / support) higher is better

\(\max(0,\, 1 - \operatorname{FVU})\) on either set of points. \(R^2\) itself has no lower bound: one answer that diverges would decide the mean of a whole catalog. The floor is the mean predictor, an answer every method can always return, so an answer worse than that counts 0 and the metric has a mean. The FVU keeps the full range and is summarized by its median.

Success

Prediction success rate higher is better

The fraction of problems for which the method produced any usable prediction at all: decoding, parsing, compiling, and constant fitting all completed and returned an evaluable expression. This is the denominator-integrity metric behind the failure convention above: where success dips, the rate metrics absorb the failures as zeros and the diagnostic metrics thin out (visible as shrinking n in the tooltip).

Symbolic similarity

These metrics compare the predicted skeleton \(\hat{\tau}\) to the ground-truth skeleton \(\bar{\tau}\) as token sequences and trees. They are computed on raw prefix skeletons: the predictions are not re-normalized to a common simplified form, so levels are offset between method families and the signal is the trend within a series.

These metrics compare the predicted skeleton \(\hat{\tau}\) to the law's skeleton \(\bar{\tau}\). Every prediction is first mapped into the law's variable names and simplified into the same canonical form, so the levels are comparable across methods.

Symbolic recovery (SRR) higher is better

$$ \mathrm{SRR} \;=\; \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbb{1}\!\left[ \operatorname{canon}\big(\hat{f}^{(k)}\big) = \operatorname{canon}\big(f^{(k)}\big) \right] $$

The prediction \(\hat{f}\) and the law \(f\) have the same certified canonical form (SimpliPy, float64) once both are simplified: structurally the same law, with constants matched up to the judge's tolerance. A re-ordered sum or an unsimplified sub-term still counts, and a close numerical fit of another shape does not. Failed predictions count as 0.

Exact skeleton match (raw) higher is better

$$ \mathrm{SRR}_{\mathrm{raw}} \;=\; \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbb{1}\!\left[ \hat{\tau}^{(k)} = \bar{\tau}^{(k)} \right] $$

Token-for-token equality of the two prefix skeletons. This is the most conservative symbolic criterion on this page: a prediction that is mathematically identical but written differently (e.g. re-ordered commutative operands, an unsimplified sub-term) does not count. It is a raw-form variant of the paper's Symbolic Recovery Rate, which re-simplifies both sides before comparing. Failed predictions count as 0.

Skeleton token F1 / precision / recall higher is better

$$ P = \frac{|\mathcal{T}(\hat{\tau}) \cap \mathcal{T}(\bar{\tau})|}{|\mathcal{T}(\hat{\tau})|}, \qquad R = \frac{|\mathcal{T}(\hat{\tau}) \cap \mathcal{T}(\bar{\tau})|}{|\mathcal{T}(\bar{\tau})|}, \qquad F_1 = \frac{2PR}{P + R} $$

Overlap of the sets of unique tokens \(\mathcal{T}(\cdot)\) used by the two skeletons: did the prediction use the right operators, variables, and constants at all? Precision penalizes inventing tokens the ground truth does not use; recall penalizes missing tokens it does use. Being set-based, these ignore token order and multiplicity: they measure vocabulary agreement, not structure. \(F_1\) is defined as 0 when both \(P\) and \(R\) are 0.

Variable-set F1 / precision / recall higher is better

The same three quantities over the sets of input variables that the prediction and the law use: precision falls when a prediction leans on a variable the law ignores, recall when it leaves out one the law needs.

Skeleton edit distance (normalized) lower is better

$$ d_{\mathrm{edit}}(\hat{\tau}, \bar{\tau}) \;=\; \frac{\operatorname{Levenshtein}(\hat{\tau}, \bar{\tau})}{\max(|\hat{\tau}|, |\bar{\tau}|)} \;\in\; [0, 1] $$

The Levenshtein distance between the two prefix token sequences (minimum number of token insertions, deletions, and substitutions to turn one into the other), normalized by the longer length. Unlike the set-based F1, this is order- and count-sensitive: it measures how much of the sequence structure is wrong, not just the vocabulary.

Tree edit distance (ZSS) lower is better

The Zhang–Shasha tree edit distance between the expression trees obtained by parsing the two prefix skeletons: the minimum total cost of node insertions, deletions, and relabelings that transforms one tree into the other. In this implementation the operations are weighted by the character-level string distance of the node labels (inserting or deleting a node costs the length of its label; relabeling costs the string edit distance between the two labels), so long-named tokens like arccosh or <constant> weigh more than single-character ones, and the value is unnormalized label-edit cost, not a node count. Because it works on the parsed tree rather than the flat token sequence, it respects the expression structure: swapping a deeply nested operator costs the same as swapping a shallow one, and subtree moves are scored more faithfully than by string edits.

Complexity and parsimony

MDL ratio (pred / law) 1 is ideal

$$ \frac{L(\hat{f})}{L(f)} $$

The description length \(L\) of the prediction over the law's, in bits, both priced in the certified canonical form: operators, variables and the digits a constant needs. Unlike a token count it charges a long constant more than a short one. 1 means as long as the law, and the median is taken on the \(\log_2\) scale.

Expression length ratio (pred/GT) 1 is ideal

$$ \rho \;=\; \frac{|\hat{\tau}|}{|\bar{\tau}|} $$

The length of the predicted skeleton relative to the ground truth, in prefix tokens. A ratio of 1 means the prediction is exactly as concise as the true expression; values above 1 flag verbose, over-parameterized predictions (a common failure mode: fitting the data with a longer expression than necessary), values below 1 flag predictions that are too simple to be the true law. This is the second headline metric because it tracks interpretability, the point of symbolic regression: a recovered-looking fit made of twice as many terms is a worse scientific answer.

Length ratio |log2| error lower is better

$$ \left| \log_2 \rho \right| \;=\; \left| \log_2 \tfrac{|\hat{\tau}|}{|\bar{\tau}|} \right| $$

The symmetric version of the length ratio: doubling and halving the true length are equally wrong (both give 1), and 0 means exactly matched length. Useful when averaging, where over- and under-shoots of the raw ratio would otherwise partially cancel.

Predicted / GT skeleton length lower is more concise

The raw prefix-token lengths \(|\hat{\tau}|\) and \(|\bar{\tau}|\) behind the ratio. The ground-truth length is a property of the benchmark (constant per expression, useful as a reference line); the predicted length shows how expression size scales with compute, noise, or data availability.

Constant-count ratio / delta (pred vs GT) 1 / 0 is ideal

$$ \frac{c(\hat{\tau})}{c(\bar{\tau})} \qquad\text{and}\qquad c(\hat{\tau}) - c(\bar{\tau}), $$

where \(c(\cdot)\) counts the <constant> tokens in a skeleton. A positive delta means the prediction uses excess constants: free parameters that let a structurally wrong expression bend toward the data. The ratio is only defined for ground truths that contain at least one constant; the delta is defined everywhere.

Unary-nestedness delta (pred − GT) lower is better

Total nestedness counts, over each maximal chain of \(m\) directly nested unary operators, the excess \(m - 1\), summed over the expression: \(\sin(\log(x))\) contributes 1, \(\sin(\log(\operatorname{arccosh}(x)))\) contributes 2, and \(\sin(x) + \log(x)\) contributes 0. Deeply nested unary chains (\(\exp(\sin(\exp(\dots)))\)) are a hallmark of unnatural, hard-to-interpret predictions. The reported value is the difference between the predicted and ground-truth nestedness, so 0 means the prediction is as (un)nested as the true expression; this is a delta variant of the paper's Total Nestedness, which reports the predicted expression's absolute count.

Model internals (Flash-ANSR series only)

Predicted log-probability higher is better

The log-probability \(\log p(\hat{\tau} \mid X, y)\) of the selected candidate's token sequence under the model's decoder, summed over the tokens of the sequence: how likely the model itself considered the expression it ended up returning. Being a sum, its level scales with expression length. Baselines have no decoder likelihood, so their series are empty here.

Selection score and Pareto rank lower is better

$$ S(\hat{f}) \;=\; \tfrac{N}{2} \log_2 \operatorname{FVU}(y, \hat{y}) \;+\; L(\hat{f}) $$

The score by which the method chose its answer, as the method computed it. For Flash-ANSR this is a two-part code in bits: the cost of the residuals on the \(N\) support points plus the description length of the expression. It is only comparable within one method. The Pareto rank says where the chosen candidate sits on the method's own front of FVU against length; 0 is on the front.

Predicted selection score lower is better

$$ s(\hat{\tau}) \;=\; \log_{10} \operatorname{FVU}(y, \hat{y}) \;+\; \gamma \, |\hat{\tau}|, \qquad \gamma = 0.05 $$

The parsimony-penalized criterion Flash-ANSR uses to pick its final answer from the sampled candidate set: the best candidate is the one minimizing support-set log-FVU plus a complexity penalty of \(\gamma\) per token (each additional token must be bought with roughly an 11% relative FVU reduction). The value plotted is the score of the selected candidate; it is a model-internal quantity, shown for completeness and for reasoning about the selection stage.

Cost

Fit time on the reference machine (s) lower is better

Wall-clock seconds to solve one problem, from the data to the submitted answer, measured for every budget of every method on one machine over a fixed, stratified subset of 262 problems. It is an axis of the explorer rather than a metric in its menu: a method is placed on it once the reference machine has timed it.

Per-problem fit time (s) lower is better

End-to-end wall-clock seconds to solve one problem: for Flash-ANSR that spans encoding the data, decoding candidates, constant fitting, and candidate selection; for the baselines, their full search or inference pipeline. The median fit time of a run is also what defines the compute axis, so this metric makes the x-axis itself inspectable against noise level or support size.

Privacy & storage

This site is a static page with no analytics, no tracking, and no third-party cookies. It stores at most two small things on your device, each only after you explicitly ask for it: one first-party cookie (srbf_colors) after you change a series colour, holding your colour overrides so the chart looks the same when you return; and one localStorage entry (srbf_theme) after you pick Light or Dark with the header toggle (the default Auto setting follows your device and stores nothing). Neither contains personal data and neither is sent anywhere; the page is fully client-side. Reset all colours deletes the cookie, and switching the theme back to Auto deletes the theme entry. Because both exist solely to fulfil preferences you explicitly request and carry no tracking, they are functional/strictly-necessary storage under the GDPR / ePrivacy Directive and require no consent banner; this notice is provided for transparency.
